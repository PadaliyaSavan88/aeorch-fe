A multi-site dashboard for agencies tracks one score per client site in a single view, sorted to surface which sites regressed since the last scan, so the agency can triage attention across a client portfolio instead of remembering to manually re-check each site individually.

## Where Manual Scanning Breaks Down

One client, one manual scan a month, is manageable. Fifteen clients, fifteen manual scans a month, each one dependent on someone remembering to run it, is where the process quietly stops happening. The failure mode isn't dramatic, it's just that site number twelve doesn't get checked in March, and nobody notices until the client asks why their score has been flat for two months.

## What a Dashboard Needs to Surface

- **One score per site**, not a full report per site, since the dashboard's job is triage, not detail.
- **Sorted by what changed**, so a site that dropped ten points since last scan is visible immediately, not buried alphabetically between two sites that are fine.
- **A last-scanned timestamp per site**, so a site that silently stopped being tracked is obvious rather than invisible.

This mirrors a pattern agencies already know from [Google Search Console's own domain property grouping](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9008080): once you're responsible for more than a couple of properties, a single rolled-up view stops being a convenience and becomes the only way the work stays consistent.

## Answer Box: How Is a Dashboard Different From a Client Report?

The dashboard is the agency's internal working view across every client at once; the report is a single client's own detailed breakdown, branded and sent to them specifically. A dashboard trying to double as a client-facing report either shows too little detail to be useful internally or too much noise to be useful externally.

## The Compounding Value of Consistency

The value of AEO and GEO tracking comes from the trend line, not any single score. A site scanned once, six months ago, tells an agency almost nothing about whether recent work helped. A site scanned monthly for six months tells a complete story: what regressed, what improved, and which fix actually moved the number. A dashboard is what makes that consistency achievable across a whole client portfolio rather than just the one or two accounts an agency has bandwidth to manually track closely.

## Key Takeaways

- Manual per-client scanning works for one or two accounts and breaks down past that
- A dashboard's job is triage: one score per site, sorted by what changed, not full detail per site
- The trend line across months is the real value, and consistency is what a dashboard makes achievable at scale
