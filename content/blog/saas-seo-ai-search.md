SaaS buying research is unusually comparison-driven — "best tool for X" and "X vs Y" are exactly the kind of queries AI assistants are well-suited to answer directly, summarizing multiple options in one response. That changes what SaaS content needs to prioritize.

## Why SaaS Is Especially Exposed to AI-Answer Behavior

Software buyers frequently start with an explicitly comparative question rather than a single-product search. An AI assistant answering "best project management tool for small teams" can name and briefly compare several options in one response — collapsing what used to be several individual search-result clicks into a single synthesized answer. Being one of the options named, with your differentiators represented accurately, is now a distinct visibility goal from just ranking for your own product name.

## Write Your Own Fair Comparisons

Counterintuitively, one of the strongest moves is publishing honest comparison content yourself — including genuine trade-offs, not just favorable framing. Content that acknowledges where a competitor is a better fit for a specific use case reads as more credible, and credibility is exactly the GEO signal that correlates with AI systems choosing to reference a source. A page that's transparently one-sided marketing gives an AI assistant less confidence to draw from it directly.

## Make Pricing Structured and Findable

Pricing hidden behind a "Contact Sales" form gives an AI assistant nothing concrete to extract or compare. Where feasible, structured, visible pricing tiers are both more transparent to buyers and more usable by systems attempting to answer "how much does X cost" or "what's the cheapest option for Y" directly.

## Structure Feature Comparisons for Extraction

A features table is naturally AEO-friendly if it's actual HTML (or schema-backed) content rather than an image or JavaScript-rendered widget an AI crawler can't parse. Keep comparison tables as real, crawlable markup:

| Feature | Your Product | Common Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Free tier | 20 scans/month | Often none |
| Setup time | Minutes | Hours |

## Use Case and Integration Pages Answer Specific Questions

"Does X integrate with Y?" and "Is X good for [specific use case]?" are exactly the kind of direct, answerable questions AEO's Q&A format and FAQPage schema are built for. Dedicated integration and use-case pages, structured this way, are more likely to be the source an AI assistant draws its answer from.
