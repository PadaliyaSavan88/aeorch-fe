Improving a low GEO score means systematically closing the gap on the specific signals large language models use to judge trustworthiness — content depth, authorship, dates, citations, and fluency. Here are nine tactics, ordered roughly by typical impact-to-effort ratio.

## 1. Add Named Authorship to Every Page

If your content currently has no byline, or a generic "Admin" attribution, this is the fastest fix available — add a real name or organization to every substantive page. It's a same-day change with no content rewrite required.

## 2. Add Machine-Readable Publication Dates

Visible dates help human readers; `Article` schema's `datePublished`/`dateModified` fields make dates machine-readable for AI systems. Add both together — visible text plus schema markup — rather than one without the other.

## 3. Expand Genuinely Thin Pages

Pages under roughly 300 words often lack room for the citations, statistics, and definitional clarity that GEO research associates with higher citation rates. Prioritize expanding pages that already get some traffic rather than every thin page equally.

## 4. Add At Least One External Citation Per Page

Link to a credible, relevant external source — research, official documentation, a recognized authority — backing a specific claim on the page. This is a GEO-specific signal distinct from SEO backlinks, since it's about what *you* cite outward, not what links inward.

## 5. Lead With a Clear Definitional Statement

Open substantive pages with a direct "X is..." or "To do Y, you need to..." statement rather than a scene-setting introduction. This helps both AEO extractability and GEO's fluency/clarity signal simultaneously.

## 6. Improve Writing Fluency

GEO research specifically identified fluency — clear, well-structured, non-repetitive writing — as a factor increasing citation rates. Avoid keyword-stuffed phrasing in favor of natural, direct sentences.

## 7. Add Statistics and Specific Data Points

Concrete numbers ("40% increase," "300-word minimum") read as more citable and verifiable than vague claims ("significantly more," "much better"). Where you have real data, state it specifically.

## 8. Consolidate Duplicate or Near-Duplicate Content

Multiple thin pages covering the same narrow topic dilute your authority on that topic compared to one comprehensive, well-cited page. Consider merging near-duplicates rather than maintaining several shallow versions.

## 9. Keep Content Updated, Not Just Dated

Adding a `dateModified` timestamp without actually updating the content is a weak signal on its own — periodically revisit and genuinely refresh older pages (updated stats, current examples) so the freshness signal reflects real currency.

## Prioritizing These Nine

If you can only do three, do #1, #2, and #4 — authorship, dates, and citations are the fastest to implement across an entire site and address three of the four core GEO signal categories directly.
