Google AI Overviews generate AI-written summary answers above the traditional results list, synthesizing multiple sources with citation links — and for many queries, they mean searchers get their answer without clicking through to any website. Here's how they work and what actually correlates with being cited.

## What Changed From SGE to AI Overviews

Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE) launched as an opt-in Labs experiment in 2023, then rolled out broadly as "AI Overviews" in May 2024, appearing for a large share of US searches within weeks of launch ([Search Engine Land, 2024](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-impressions-data-443055)). The core mechanic is unchanged: for qualifying queries, Google generates a synthesized answer, cites the sources it drew from, and displays this above the traditional blue-link results.

## The Traffic Trade-Off

For queries where the Overview fully answers the question, click-through to any individual source tends to drop — the searcher already has their answer. But for sites that *are* cited within the Overview, the citation itself is a visibility event, and the citation link can still drive traffic from users who want more detail than the summary provided. The practical goal shifts from "rank #1" to "be one of the cited sources" — a related but distinct target.

## What Correlates With Being Cited

Google hasn't published an exact ranking formula for Overview inclusion, but observed patterns align closely with AEO and GEO signals more broadly:

| Factor | Why it likely helps |
|---|---|
| Direct, concise answers near the top of a page | Easier for the summarization system to extract cleanly |
| FAQPage / HowTo schema | Structures Q&A and step-by-step content explicitly |
| Clear authorship and dates | Signals a credible, current source worth citing |
| Strong existing Search ranking | Overviews draw heavily from already well-ranked, indexed pages |

Notably, strong traditional SEO fundamentals remain a prerequisite — Overviews aren't a separate track that bypasses ranking; they're generated substantially from pages that already rank well.

## Should You Try to Avoid Being Included?

Some publishers have debated blocking Google-Extended to opt out of AI training and Overview inclusion entirely, trading potential citation visibility for keeping all traffic within traditional clicks. For most sites, the better-supported strategy is the opposite: optimize to be cited, since exclusion doesn't stop the Overview from appearing for that query — it just means a competitor gets cited instead of you.

## Key Takeaways

- AI Overviews synthesize answers from multiple sources and cite them, reducing but not eliminating click-through

- Overview inclusion correlates with strong existing SEO plus AEO/GEO signals — not a separate ranking system
