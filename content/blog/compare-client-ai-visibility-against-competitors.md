Agencies compare a client's AI visibility against competitors by running the same scored scan (SEO, AEO, GEO, Authority, AI Compatibility) against each competitor's URL and placing the results side by side, metric by metric. The comparison is the point, not the absolute number.

## Why Relative Position Beats an Absolute Score

Tell a client their AEO score is 62 and the reasonable next question is "is that good?" Tell them their score is 62 against a named competitor's 41 and their own top competitor's 78, and the conversation moves straight to "what does the 78 do that we don't." A comparison table converts an abstract number into a competitive gap, which is a much easier thing to sell a project against.

## What to Actually Compare

- **Overall score**, for the one-line headline.
- **The five category breakdown** (SEO, AEO, GEO, Authority, AI Compatibility), since a client might lead on SEO and trail badly on AEO, and that's the exact insight a single blended number hides.
- **The specific gap driving the difference**, not just the numbers. "They lead on AEO because they have FAQPage schema on every service page and you don't" is a fix; "they lead on AEO" is not.

## Answer Box: Which Competitors Should Go in the Comparison?

The two or three businesses the client actually loses deals to, chosen by the client, not by search volume or brand size. A comparison against an irrelevant category leader produces an impressive-looking but useless gap. The right comparison set makes the report a genuine competitive tool rather than a vanity chart.

## The Boundary This Requires

Scanning a competitor's site to get a comparison score means analyzing a site you don't own, and that comes with a real limit worth being explicit about: the comparison should expose scores and category breakdowns only, never a full detailed report of the competitor's site. An agency handing a client a full audit of a competitor's site (issue-by-issue, with generated fixes) has gone from "benchmarking" to distributing a detailed teardown of someone else's property, which is a different and murkier thing entirely.

## Key Takeaways

- A score means little alone; the same score next to a named competitor's is what drives action
- Compare all five categories, not just the overall number, since the gap usually hides in one dimension
- Client-chosen, relevant competitors beat category leaders picked for name recognition
