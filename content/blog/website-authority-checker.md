A website authority checker measures the trust signals that determine whether search engines — and increasingly AI models — treat your site as a credible source worth citing. It's broader than backlink-based Domain Authority scores, and it's one of the fastest things to improve once you know what's missing.

## What "Authority" Means Beyond Backlinks

Third-party Domain Authority metrics are built almost entirely from backlink profiles — how many sites link to you, and how authoritative those linking sites are. That's one real signal, but it's slow to build and outside your direct control. A broader authority check looks at what you *can* control directly:

| Signal | What it checks |
|---|---|
| Trust pages | Do About, Contact, and Privacy pages exist and contain real information? |
| Author transparency | Are content pages attributed to a named person or organization? |
| External validation | Do credible external sites reference or link to you? |
| Organizational schema | Is `Organization` or `Person` schema present to formalize who's behind the site? |
| Consistency | Does contact information match across your site and external profiles? |

## Why AI Models Weigh Authority Signals

When an LLM decides whether to reference a source in a generated answer, it's implicitly asking "how confident should I be in this?" A site with no visible author, no contact information, and no external validation gives a model nothing to anchor that confidence to — regardless of how well-written the content itself is. This is distinct from GEO's content-depth signals; authority is specifically about *who's behind the content*, not how the content itself is structured.

## The Fastest Authority Wins

Unlike backlinks, which take months to accumulate, these are same-day fixes:

1. **Write a real About page.** Who are you, what do you do, why should someone trust you? Avoid generic placeholder copy.
2. **Make Contact information genuinely findable.** A real email or contact form, not just a buried mailto link.
3. **Add author bios.** A named author with a short credibility statement (role, expertise) on every substantive content page.
4. **Add Organization schema.** Mark up your company name, logo, and contact details in JSON-LD so it's machine-readable, not just visually present.

## What a Checker Flags

Running an automated authority check surfaces gaps you might not notice browsing your own site casually — a Privacy page that 404s, a Contact form with no working submit handler, or blog posts with no byline at all.
