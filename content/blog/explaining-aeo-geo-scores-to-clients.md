Explaining AEO and GEO scores to a non-technical client works best with a fixed structure repeated every call: one plain-language analogy, the score in context against last month, and one specific fix, in that order, every time. Consistency does more work than any single clever explanation.

## The Analogy That Actually Lands

Traditional SEO is being findable in a library's card catalog: search for the right term and the book turns up. AEO is being the passage the librarian actually reads aloud when someone asks a question directly. A site can be well cataloged and still never get read aloud if it isn't structured for that second job. This analogy survives repetition better than most, because it maps directly onto the difference between ranking and citation without requiring any technical vocabulary at all.

## The Structure That Survives Every Call

- **The score in context**: not just the number, but "up from 51 last month" or "flat for two months, here's why."
- **One plain-language cause**: "ChatGPT can't read your FAQ page because it's missing a tag that tells AI models where the questions and answers are," not "missing FAQPage schema."
- **One specific next step**: a single fix, not a list, since a client remembers one action item far more reliably than five.

This is the same discipline behind the [US government's plain language guidelines](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/): define the unfamiliar term the moment it's used, in the same sentence, rather than assuming the reader already knows it or making them look it up separately.

## Answer Box: How Do You Define Jargon Without Sounding Condescending?

Define it in the same clause it's first used, not in a separate glossary the client won't read: "your robots.txt file, the instructions that tell AI crawlers what they're allowed to read, is currently blocking GPTBot." The definition is doing work inline, not interrupting the explanation to teach a vocabulary lesson.

## Why Repetition Beats a Better Explanation

A client hears "AEO" for the first time in month one and forgets it by month three, not from lack of intelligence, but because it's not their job to remember it between calls. Repeating the same short analogy every month, even briefly, costs ten seconds and prevents the much larger cost of a client who's quietly lost the thread and stops engaging with the report at all.

## Key Takeaways

- One consistent analogy, repeated, beats a new explanation every month
- Define jargon inline, in the same sentence it's used, not in a separate glossary
- Structure every call the same way: score in context, one cause, one next step
