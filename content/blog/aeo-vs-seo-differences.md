AEO and SEO share some underlying tactics — clean structure, clear content, crawlability — but they optimize for genuinely different outcomes. Here are seven concrete differences that matter when deciding where to focus.

## 1. The Goal Itself

SEO's goal is ranking in a list of results a user then chooses from. AEO's goal is being the direct answer a user receives, often without seeing a list at all. This changes what "success" looks like: a #3 SEO ranking is a good outcome; being extracted as the AEO answer is closer to a #1-and-only outcome for that query.

## 2. The Content Format That Wins

SEO rewards comprehensive, long-form content that covers a topic exhaustively — more depth generally helps rank for more related terms. AEO rewards concise, direct answers positioned early, often in Q&A format. A page can be excellent for SEO and mediocre for AEO if its best answer is buried in paragraph four instead of stated plainly near the top.

## 3. Structured Data Requirements

SEO benefits from schema markup but doesn't require specific types — Organization, Product, or Review schema all help in different contexts. AEO specifically depends on FAQPage, HowTo, and Article schema, since these are the formats answer engines are built to parse and extract from directly.

## 4. What "Ranking #1" Even Means

| | SEO | AEO |
|---|---|---|
| Success metric | Position in results list | Being the cited/quoted answer |
| Multiple winners? | Yes — top 10 all get some traffic | Often just one source gets quoted per answer |
| Personalization | Heavy (location, history, device) | Less personalized — more consistent across users |

## 5. The Role of Backlinks

Backlinks are a core SEO ranking factor — more authoritative links generally mean better rankings. AEO's citation decision leans more on structural and content-trust signals (schema, authorship, directness) than on backlink count specifically, though backlinks still contribute to the broader authority picture.

## 6. Crawler Requirements

SEO depends on Googlebot and Bingbot access. AEO depends on a wider, newer set of crawlers — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot — that many sites haven't explicitly configured for, since `robots.txt` files often predate these bots entirely.

## 7. How You Measure Progress

SEO progress is measurable through rank tracking and organic traffic in analytics. AEO progress is harder to measure directly — there's no universal "AEO analytics" dashboard yet — which is why structural scoring (schema presence, crawler access, heading format) is currently the most practical proxy for AEO readiness.

## Why the Differences Matter in Practice

Treating AEO as "just more SEO" leads teams to keep writing longer articles and building more backlinks while never adding FAQPage schema or checking whether GPTBot can even reach the site — missing the specific, different levers AEO actually depends on.

## Key Takeaways

- SEO optimizes for ranking position; AEO optimizes for being the extracted/cited answer
- AEO depends on specific schema types (FAQPage, HowTo, Article) and a wider set of AI crawlers
- Strong SEO doesn't automatically produce strong AEO — they need to be checked and built separately
